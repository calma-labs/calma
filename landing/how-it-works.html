The mechanics behind Calma.

A look under the hood: the capital-efficiency design, the two mechanisms behind it, and the live rate engine.

Why Calma

Capital efficiency, by construction.

Calma is a Solana lending protocol where the rate curve and price feeds are fully customizable. Its withdraw queue lets a pool run close to fully lent without stranding lenders, so utilization stays high and lend/borrow spreads stay tight: less idle capital, more yield for lenders, and cheaper borrowing.

03 · Predictability

Fixed rates, by design.

When a market chooses fixed pricing, a time-scheduled rate model pins borrow and lend rates for the term of a position, independent of utilization. Predictable cost, predictable yield, no rate surprises.

How it works

2 mechanisms, 1 goal:
maximum capital efficiency.

Together they push utilization toward its safe maximum without sacrificing liquidity or stable pricing.

M1

Withdraw Queue

The problem

Pools that promise instant exits must hold an idle liquidity buffer to cover withdrawals. That reserve never earns, which caps how high utilization can safely run.

How Calma fixes it

Calma orders withdrawals in a queue that clears as loans repay. No standby buffer needed, so the pool can run closer to fully lent without a liquidity crunch.

M2

Modular Rate Program

The problem

In most protocols the rate model is hard-wired into the lending contract: one curve, unchangeable without redeploying, and blind to anything but instantaneous utilization.

How Calma fixes it

Calma's rate model is a separate program each market plugs into. By default it reprices against live utilization, steering supply and demand back to the efficient target so more of the pool stays lent; markets that need certainty can swap in a fixed, time-scheduled model instead.

Live rate engine

Rates that move in real time.

Utilization is just one input. Because the rate engine is modular, it can price on time, oracle feeds, or anything else you plug in, not just how much of the pool is borrowed. Time-averaged utilization resists manipulation, schedules enable fixed-term rates, and oracles let pricing track conditions beyond the pool.
